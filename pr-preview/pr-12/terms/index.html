4. Using LeaseMate

LeaseMate lets customers manage properties and rooms, leads, inspections, deposits and bonds, and prepare room flatmate agreements and whole-house leases (Consumer Affairs Victoria Form 1) and send them for electronic signature. Written room sublets use LeaseMate’s Flatmate Agreement until a lawyer decides whether Victorian law requires the prescribed Form 1 for those agreements. Bed (shared room) lets are not available.

Applications to rent a Victorian property are made using the prescribed rental application form (Form 3A).

LeaseMate applies the Victorian maximum bond: the bond cannot be more than one month’s rent, and there is no cap when weekly rent is over $900. The same rule applies to sublets.

We may change, add or remove features over time. We will try to give reasonable notice of changes that significantly reduce what LeaseMate does.

13. Data export and deletion on termination

  • If you close your customer account, we offer you an export of your Customer Data and start a 7-day grace period. You can cancel the closure during those 7 days.
  • When the grace period ends, we delete your Customer Data from LeaseMate’s live systems, including any owner summaries, except records that are on a legal hold (see below). Copies in our encrypted backups are removed as those backups are automatically deleted, which takes up to 35 days. Until then, backups are only used to restore the service, and we do not restore deleted customer or person data from them. Operational and system logs are kept for 30 days. See our Privacy Policy for details.
  • Legal holds. Victorian law requires identified renter information to be destroyed or permanently de-identified within 3 years after a tenancy ends, unless a law or a court or tribunal order requires it to be kept, or the renter has agreed in writing. We therefore keep identified tenancy records for up to 3 years after a tenancy ends, then de-identify them, except where a specific hold applies: RTA_RENT_RECORD (rent payment records, at least 12 months); RTA_DEFAULT_MAX (identified renter data, and other lead and tenant data, destroyed or de-identified 3 years after the tenancy ends); TAX_RECORD (tax records kept until 7 years after the tenancy ends, in de-identified form; identified only if the owner confirms they are needed for tax); DISPUTE_OR_PROCEEDING (until the dispute or proceeding is resolved); COURT_ORDER; and RENTER_CONSENT. Held records stay encrypted and are visible only to that customer’s owners and admins (not house managers). 30 days before a hold ends and these records are deleted or de-identified, we prompt the customer’s owners and admins to export them. Identity documents follow section 7 of the Privacy Policy and are never placed on a legal hold.
  • Owner summary. After those held records are deleted or de-identified, we may keep a small summary of the tenancy for the customer: the property, the tenancy start and end dates, and the rent. It has no renter names, contact details or ID. Only that customer’s owners can see it, not admins, house managers or lease holders. It is deleted 5 years after the owner records the property as sold. We prompt the owner to export it 30 days before that deletion. If no sale is recorded, we ask the owner every 5 years whether the property has been sold. The summary is also included in whole-customer deletion.
  • An owner or admin can delete one person’s information from the customer’s account after a two-step confirmation, except that deletion is blocked while any of that person’s records are on a legal hold or part of an open dispute. Where deletion is allowed, the action is audited and takes effect straight away, subject to the legal holds above. Deleted information is not restored from backups.
  • ID documents also follow the deletion rules in our Privacy Policy (12 months after move-out for tenants; for unsuccessful applicants, 30 days after rejection or 30 days after the room’s agreement is signed, whichever is earlier, or up to 6 months with the applicant’s written consent) and are never placed on a legal hold.
